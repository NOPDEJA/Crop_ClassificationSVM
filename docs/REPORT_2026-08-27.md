## 1. Summary

Four things happened since the last report.

1. **The corrected model improved twice, and the second improvement is the real one.** The
   parcel-disjoint run went from macro F1 0.2248 to 0.2283 after I repaired the protocol
   defects the last report listed, and then to **0.2344** after I added two spectral
   columns, raised the Stage-3 kernel capacity and applied a tuned operating point. That last figure is measured on
   5,500,269 test pixels that no model in the chain has ever seen.

2. **I found that our own operating-point numbers were read about 0.043 too high.** The
   sweep that chose the operating point selected on fold 1's tuning half and then reported
   that same half, so it was reporting a number it had optimised. I rebuilt the procedure to
   select on the calibration half and report the tuning half once, and on the same model the
   score drops from 0.2720 to 0.2294. Any operating point quoted from the old script
   reads high by roughly that much.

3. **The cost-sensitive run I proposed does not work, and I stated the gate before I ran
   it.** Tempered class weights scored 0.2272 on the tuning half against 0.2294 for the
   model without them, so the gate stayed shut and I did not read the test fold for it. It
   did keep one more crop alive, which is the only thing it bought.

4. **The collaborator now has a composed cascade**, which our §6 last time said they did
   not. That claim was written from their repository at an earlier commit and it is out of
   date. Their chain also has one property worth naming, which is that the pixels they
   evaluate on are drawn from the same ground their models were fitted on.

The unflattering summary is that two of the four items are corrections to things I wrote or
proposed myself, and the model gained 0.0096 macro F1 in total.

## 3. The problem I am actually fighting

The class imbalance in this tile is 6,685 to 1 between rubber and Langsat. Rubber has
3.24 M test pixels and Langsat has 191, and Langsat has 13 validation pixels in the whole
of fold 1. So macro F1, which is an unweighted mean over thirteen classes, is dominated by
classes I have almost no data for.

What last report established, and what this report keeps finding, is that the dial which
moves this number is not the architecture. It is how priors are handled at decision time and
which population the score is computed over. Three measurements from the same trained M5
cascade, differing only in those two things:

| what varies | macro F1 |
|---|---|
| plain argmax at inference | 0.2140 |
| operating point (0.3, 0.6) chosen on held-out rows | 0.2294 |
| their 200k-per-class population, 14 labels including `others` | 0.3078 |

The first two are the tuning half of fold 1 and the third is the test fold under the
collaborator's protocol, so they are not three readings of one quantity, but the size of
each effect is the point. Moving the decision rule is worth about 0.015 and changing the
evaluation population is worth about 0.03 to 0.04, and neither of those touched the model at
all. Nothing I have trained this year has moved macro F1 by 0.03.

That is why I keep predeclaring what counts as success before running anything. With a dial
this powerful sitting next to the metric, it is very easy to report a tuning artefact as a
result, and I have now caught myself doing exactly that once, in §4 below.

## 4. What I tried this week, and it did not work

### The idea

If the caps alone leave the rare crops starved, then a cost-sensitive SVM should help. This
is a standard remedy in remote sensing (Waske et al. 2009 for cost-sensitive SVM in land
cover, and the RUESVMs work of 2020 for the imbalanced-crop case), and the natural partner
to the post-hoc prior correction family (Saerens et al. 2002) that the operating point in
§2 comes from. So I changed exactly one thing against M5 and ran it overnight, from 02:29 to
05:48 on 2026-08-26, three hours nineteen minutes.

The scheme is tempered rather than full inverse frequency. Each class gets
w = sqrt(n_max / n), computed from the counts of the model actually being fitted, after the
caps have been applied. Full inverse frequency at 6,685:1 would give a handful of Langsat
pixels the same total weight as all of rubber, and the operating-point sweep had already
shown that pushing all the way to uniform costs more than it buys.

### The gate, written down before the run

I wrote the predeclaration into `runs/s2_2018_3date_parcel_weighted/PREDECLARATION.md`
before launching, and the run itself was launched with `SKIP_TEST=1` so that it could not
read the test fold even if I wanted it to. The rule was that the operating point would be
selected on fold 1's calibration half over a 169-cell grid and scored once on the tuning
half, and only if that beat M5's number from the identical procedure would the test fold be
read.

### What happened

| | M5 | weighted | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| cell selected on the calibration half | (0.3, 0.6) | (0.3, 0.6) | same cell |
| **tuning-half macro F1** | **0.2294** | **0.2272** | **-0.0022** |
| crops scoring at least 0.01 | 10 of 13 | 11 of 13 | +1 |

The gate stayed shut, so **the test fold was not read for this run** and 0.2344 remains the
number. The one thing the weights bought is Longan, which went from 0.0029 to 0.0130 on the
tuning half, and the cost was spread thinly over Rambutan, Jackfruit and Mangosteen.

### Why it did so little, which I did not anticipate

When I read the weights the run logged, most of them were 1.00:

| model | weights |
|---|---|
| Stage 2, four groups | all four exactly 1.00 |
| Stage-3 field | all three exactly 1.00 |
| Stage-3 plantation | rubber 1.00, oil palm 1.00, coconut 2.90 |
| Stage-3 orchards | durian 1.00 up to Langsat 6.54 |

The reason is that the caps had already done the balancing. Stage 2 caps every group at
200,000 and all four groups have more rows than that, so after capping the four counts are
identical and a weight computed from post-cap counts is exactly 1. The same thing happens in
the field expert, where rice, cassava and pineapple all hit the 70,000 per-crop cap. So the
experiment I described as "class weights on Stage 2 and all three Stage-3 experts" was in
practice class weights on the orchards expert plus coconut, and everywhere else it was a
no-op by construction.

That is a real finding rather than a bug, and it is the more interesting half of the
negative result. **The cap schedule already is our class-weighting scheme.** Anywhere the
cap binds for every class in a model, weights computed after capping have nothing left to
correct, and the only place they can act is where a class is too rare to reach its cap. Any
future work on imbalance here has to go at the caps, not at `class_weight`.

### A second negative result

I also ran the tree-merge probe, which collapses orchards and plantation into one Stage-2
subclass so that the misroute that kills the orchard crops becomes harmless. The cascade
error analysis predicted large recall gains from it. On the tuning half it scores **0.2158**
against M5's **0.2283** at the same fixed operating point, so it is worse, and the
prediction was wrong. It was a reasonable bet from the routing table and it did not pay.

## 5. Analysis

### The measurement error in our own sweep

`m2_operating_point.py` selected the operating point by maximising macro F1 on fold 1's
tuning half, and then reported the tuning half. That is selecting on the number you report,
over a 169-cell grid, and it is optimistic for the same reason any grid search is optimistic
on its own selection set. I rebuilt it as `sweep_operating_point.py`, which selects on the
calibration half and scores the selected cell once on the tuning half.

On M5 the same grid gives 0.2720 on the calibration half and **0.2294** on the tuning half,
so the old procedure was reading **+0.0426** high. The honest trade is that selection is now
in-sample to the rows the Platt sigmoids were fitted on, but the reported number is not
in-sample to anything, and I would rather have that. Both runs in §4 happen to select the
same cell (0.3, 0.6), which at least says the choice transfers between two disjoint parcel
samples.

This does not touch the 0.2344 test figure, which was always measured on the untouched test
fold. It touches every fold-1 number we have quoted from the old script.

### Feature importance

Permutation importance on M5, over 150,000 validation rows, three shuffles per column, on
Stage 1 and on the orchards expert. Values are the drop in macro F1 when a column is
shuffled, summed over the three dates, so a larger number means the model needed it more.

| index | Stage 1 | orchards expert |
|---|---|---|
| MSAVI | **0.372** | **0.205** |
| BSI | **0.372** | 0.172 |
| EVI | 0.266 | 0.117 |
| NDWI | 0.253 | 0.104 |
| NDBI | 0.201 | 0.100 |
| **B11** | 0.194 | 0.083 |
| SWIR_RATIO | 0.179 | 0.087 |
| NDVI | 0.142 | 0.069 |
| SWIR_NIR | 0.043 | 0.067 |
| **MTCI** | **0.023** | **0.039** |

Baselines are macro F1 0.6422 over four superclasses for Stage 1, and 0.2580 over the seven
orchard crops for the expert.

The answer to the question I actually asked is not the one I wanted. **MTCI is last in both
models**, and it is last by a wide margin at Stage 1. I added MTCI and raw B11 in M3 partly
because the collaborator's feature set has MTCI and ours did not, and B11 does earn a
middling place, but MTCI does close to nothing here. What makes that verdict harder to
dismiss is that MTCI is the only red-edge-derived column we have, so it has no correlated
substitute to hide behind. B11's number is the opposite case and is probably understated,
because SWIR_RATIO and SWIR_NIR are built from the same SWIR bands and can cover for it.

The other thing I did not expect is that the two soil and moisture indices, MSAVI and BSI, top
both models and beat NDVI by more than two to one. For separating perennial tree crops that
are green in all three dates, how bare the ground underneath looks carries more than how
green the canopy is, and that is a sensible thing for the data to be telling us.

Two dates of the same index carry each other, so a single-column permutation understates a
family and even these three-date sums are a lower bound. Read them as a ranking, not as a
budget.

### What is not the limitation

It is worth repeating a result from earlier work, because it keeps getting misread. The
seven crops that score near zero end to end are **not** at the limit of what these three
dates can distinguish. Given balanced priors, five dates and enough kernel capacity, the
same crops reach 0.44 to 0.68 in isolation, and Langsat, with 27 parcels, scores highest of
all. The end-to-end zeros are an engineering limit in the cascade, mostly in Stage-2
routing, and not evidence that the spectral signal is absent.

## 6. The XGBoost study, corrected

Section 6 of the last report said the collaborator's repository contained no composed
cascade. **That is now out of date.** Reading the repository at commit `a9a7ca0`
(2026-08-25), `code/inference/inference.py` chains three models, and it does it in a way
that is close to ours in spirit.

- Rows with P(water) at or above 0.56 are removed, then rows with P(building) at or above
  0.56 are removed from what is left, and the flat 14-class crop XGBoost classifies the
  survivors.
- Non-crop truth is mapped to 9999, called `others`, and their macro is over 14 labels
  including it. `others` is a class their crop model was actually trained on, capped at
  200,000 like every other class.
- Their crop report is computed on the filter survivors only.

So we have converged on the same idea from two directions, which is that false positives on
non-crop ground have to count somewhere. They handle it with a fourteenth class and we
handle it with a Stage-2 sink plus mapping non-crop truth to 0. Neither of us was doing this
three weeks ago.

### Our numbers under their protocol

I rewrote `rescore_collaborator_protocol.py` against that commit. Their building filter has
no analogue in our cascade, because buildings live inside our Stage-1 `others` superclass
along with roads and bare ground, so I report a bracket instead of pretending otherwise.

| protocol | rows | macro F1, 14 labels | macro F1, 13 crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours, strict, whole test fold | 5,500,269 | 0.2743 | **0.2344** |
| theirs, water filter only | 1,626,372 | **0.3078** | 0.2673 |
| theirs, with P(others) standing in for the building filter | 1,152,302 | 0.3060 | 0.2757 |

The stand-in drops strictly more non-crop rows than a real building filter would, so it
flatters us, and the water-only variant drops strictly fewer, so it does not. The truth sits
between the two. Population and metric definition alone are worth roughly +0.03 to +0.04
here, with the model completely unchanged.

### The two gaps that remain

1. **The 200,000-per-class cap.** It leaves rubber at 200,000 against Langsat's 191 in our
   test fold, about 1,000 to 1, instead of the tile's 6,685 to 1. Rare-class precision is
   largely a function of prevalence, so this is not a small difference in how hard the task
   is.

2. **The survivor-biased crop denominator.** Their crop metrics are computed after two
   filters have already removed rows, so the crop model is never charged for water or
   buildings that the earlier stages let through.

There is also a third thing, which is not a gap between us but a property of their setup
worth naming. **Their evaluation pixels are drawn from the ground their models trained on.**
`extract_crops.py` samples up to 200,000 eroded pixels per class for training and
`train_crops.py` splits that file 60/20/20 by row, and then `extract_all.py` independently
re-samples up to 200,000 pixels per class from the same tile for inference. Nothing
separates the two draws spatially. For every class with fewer than 200,000 pixels, and that
is every crop except rubber, the training draw is essentially the whole class, so the
inference draw lands on pixels the model was fitted on. The overlap is concentrated in
exactly the rare classes a macro average rewards most. This is the same pixel-level leakage
we found in our own protocol on 2026-08-25 and fixed with the parcel split, so I raise it as
a shared problem rather than as a criticism.

**I am still not proposing a joint table.** Until one population and one denominator are
agreed, a side-by-side would compare measuring sticks. The rescore script above is ready to
serve as the shared basis whenever we want to agree one.

## 7. Limitations

1. **The test fold has now been read three times**, for the
   baseline, M0 and M5, and it stayed unread for the weighted run because the gate shut.
   Every read costs a little of its independence, and I am treating further reads as
   expensive.
2. **Hyperparameters are still frozen** at values chosen under the old leaky pixel-level
   search. They cannot bias the test score, being fixed constants, but they may be
   suboptimal for the natural-prior regime.
3. **Fold supports vary a great deal**, because the split balances parcel counts rather than
   pixel counts. Rubber has 7.25 M training and 3.24 M test pixels. The result is honest for
   this test fold and it is specific to this fold's composition.
4. **Langsat should not be quoted per class.** It has 1,639 training, 13 validation and 191
   test pixels, and it scores 0.0000 everywhere. There is not enough of it to support any
   claim, positive or negative.
5. **M0 to M5 is not attributable**, as stated in §2, because features and the operating
   point changed together.
6. **Run-to-run noise exists at full scale.** The weighted run and M5 routed 16,832,166 and
   16,832,158 pixels into Stage 2 respectively, an eight-pixel difference from identical
   code paths, which is threaded floating point rather than the class weights. It is far too
   small to explain the -0.0022, but it means I should not read the fourth decimal place of
   anything here.
7. Labels are still the 2018 survey, and the mixed-crop compound codes are still excluded.

## 8. Next steps

In the order I would spend time on them.

1. **A cap-strategy experiment.** This follows directly from §4. Since the caps are the
   balancing mechanism, compare the current hand-picked caps against a uniform cap and
   against a cap proportional to sqrt(n), and measure on the tuning half so the test fold
   stays shut. This is the cheapest remaining idea with a real mechanism behind it.
2. **Difference-of-date features.** Rubber defoliates between October and December, which is
   the one signal in our existing acquisition window that we have never given the model
   directly (Cai et al. 2022, and Wang et al. 2023 in Nature for the regional picture). No
   new imagery is needed.
3. **Red-edge columns, with the §5 result attached.** We compute MTCI but we do not feed
   the raw red-edge bands, and the chlorophyll red-edge literature (Clevers and Gitelson
   2013) suggests they carry information MTCI compresses away. But MTCI came last in both
   permutation probes, so this is now a weaker idea than it was on Monday and I would put
   it behind items 1 and 2. If the raw bands do nothing either, red edge is finished for
   this tile and we should say so.
4. **Parcel-majority aggregation as a labelled side analysis**, not as a headline number
   (Belgiu and Csillik 2018, Matvienko et al. 2020). It changes the unit of analysis, so it
   must never be compared against a pixel-level figure, but it is what an end user of a land
   use map would actually do with the output.
5. **Permutation importance over the full cascade**, not just Stage 1 and the orchards
   expert.

For the paper's framing, Chen et al. 2024 is the reference point to cite for the ceiling.
They map crops at 10 m in eastern Thailand at 85.6 % overall accuracy, but with a dense
2017 to 2023 harmonic time series, so their number says what a long series buys and not what
three dates can reach. Foody 2020 is the reference for metric protocol, and Waśniewski et
al. 2022 with Turkoglu et al. 2021 for the hierarchical against flat question that §6 is
really about. Texture is cited but not built (Nomura and Mitchard 2018), and it stays out of
scope until the cap question is settled.

Deliberately not doing: a second retrain before the cap experiment is designed, per-crop
manual thresholds (the validation supports in limitation 4 make them untunable honestly),
and any joint table before §6's two gaps are agreed.
